# Methods for the unresolved semantic-map problems

## Expressed work versus runtime intentions

8 October 2026. [Jason's BDI tutorial](https://jason-lang.github.io/jason/tutorials/hello-bdi/readme.html) distinguishes goal adoption, concurrent intentions, suspension, resumption and dropping intentions. Its [intention inspection API](https://jason-lang.github.io/api/jason/stdlib/intention.html) can expose runtime intention state and plan stacks. That is useful for defining which questions a history should distinguish; it does not license recovering those internal states from arbitrary natural-language traces. The abstract of Cohen and Levesque's [*Intention is choice with commitment*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0004370290900555) further motivates distinguishing intention from merely mentioned desirable work. The full article was inaccessible; only its abstract was reviewed.

Our adaptation records source-attributed work declarations, without inferring internal adoption, unspoken withdrawal or verified completion. Two experiments show why local object extraction must precede that history: a report's speech-act target is different from the reported environmental work, and a single Orient can describe an earlier outcome plus a new undertaking. The replacement candidate contract retains those separately addressable objects inside the same unit and postpones identity/history. Jason's implementation was not installed or executed, and its guarantees do not validate our semantic extraction. [Experiments, failures and replacement contract](SEMANTIC_EXTRACTION_WORKFLOW.md#local-work-objects-before-history).

## Repeated sequences as an inspectable macro layer

The separated-return extension uses the same [execution-pattern paper](https://www.vdaalst.com/publications/p610.pdf), specifically its distinction between recurring contiguous blocks, tandem repetition and overlap conventions. Our own bounded suffix/LCP implementation retains disjoint separated occurrences, excludes continuous periodic intervals and keeps overlaps explicit. It does not implement the paper's Fuzzy Miner or claim its performance results. Independent brute-force substring and periodic-interval enumeration checks short sequences; a real airline review binds the resulting pattern to unchanged local units and exact-pair procedure evidence. [Implementation, evidence and limits](BEHAVIOR_MAP_INTERFACE.md#sequences-that-return-after-intervening-work).

8 October 2026. [Li, Bose and van der Aalst, *Mining Context-Dependent and Interactive Business Process Maps using Execution Patterns*](https://www.vdaalst.com/publications/p610.pdf) motivates maps that aggregate patterns and let an analyst return to the underlying events. Its pattern discussion explicitly considers overlap and abstraction granularity. We retain that separation between observed execution structure and the interpretation assigned to it; our map keeps every original unit and source gap.

[Reißner, Armas-Cervantes and La Rosa, *Efficient Conformance Checking using Approximate Alignment Computation with Tandem Repeats*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01781) exploits consecutive repeated sequences for trace compression in conformance checking. We use the tandem-repeat concept, not their alignment algorithm or performance claims. Our own bounded-period scan reports maximal periodic regions, their shortest periods and partial trailing copies. It is checked against an independent exhaustive interval-enumeration oracle on short inputs.

[PM4Py's trace-segment filter](https://github.com/process-intelligence-solutions/pm4py/blob/release/pm4py/filtering.py) makes activity and case identity explicit and distinguishes exact variants, prefixes, suffixes and contained segments. This supports defining the alphabet and ordering before comparing patterns. Our alphabets remain separate: selected immediate-work identities in the semantic map, and exact stripped command strings in the public ALFWorld source study. No package or external model was installed. Neither alphabet establishes exploration, exploitation, failure causes or conformance to an expected plan.

The first implementation covers consecutive repetition. Repeated segments separated by other work, active goal/Plan histories and state-aware strategy interpretation remain required follow-ups; they are not relabeled as completed by this detector. [Implementation and source evidence](BEHAVIOR_MAP_INTERFACE.md#repeated-work-sequences).

## Product adaptation

This design is our synthesis, not an existing method's measured result.

1. **Local contribution.** Select the coherent source span, one base activity, then readable category-conditioned content. A local gap plus immediate correction can remain one Orient contribution. A Plan requires an organizing approach/stages. Supporting context stays with the contribution. Neither noun phrases nor generated questions substitute for the unit.
2. **Readable work record.** Keep the intended work in natural language, source, actor, and expressed conditions or acceptance criteria. For example: “Exchange the first kettle for a ceramic, 2-liter, gas-compatible replacement.” Work-instance identity is separate from product IDs, paths or command strings. Preserve user requests, agent proposals and adopted work as attributed records.
3. **History update.** After local records exist, select the referenced earlier item and supported change. Candidate operations can add work, refine it, replace a specified part, withdraw it or retain it. Preserve the old version and changed scope. An agent proposal does not silently update the user's request. Returning to an earlier alternative does not duplicate work. Assess and Revise remain relations.
4. **Action and evidence map.** Link attempts to the appropriate work version and retain source dispatch/response identity. Consent requests, authorization, dispatches, returned state and completion claims remain separately inspectable. Failure does not erase an attempt or automatically close the task. Later approval cannot retroactively authorize an earlier attempt.
5. **Macro view.** Project parallel work histories with changes, attempts and feedback. Aggregate target persistence, scope changes and verification only after these links are validated. Activity frequencies alone cannot recover this structure.

The same representation can describe a test target and acceptance criterion, a search question and constraints, or a transaction and authorized scope. Framework adapters preserve source facts and execution identity; they do not redefine semantic activities. No new overlapping activity tags are required.

## Event identity and recurrence overviews

[OCEL's JSON specification](https://www.ocel-standard.org/specification/formats/json/) keeps events and objects as separate collections and qualifies their relationships. That supports one event relating to several objects without turning each association into another executed event. Our adaptation keeps one dispatched unit even when it has several requested-work, Plan or check links. It does not require replacing our existing source format with OCEL.

The inspected [PM4Py OC-DFG implementation](https://github.com/process-intelligence-solutions/pm4py/blob/release/pm4py/algo/discovery/ocel/ocdfg/variants/classic.py) separately aggregates events, unique objects and total object associations, and separately represents event pairs in edges. This is useful for a concrete denominator problem: three checking expressions linked to one operation must not count as three operations. The code was read for methodology; no package was installed and no implementation code was copied.

The resulting work-history projection counts unique selected dispatch IDs and retains their source-order positions within each actor/trajectory. Only existing positive whole-target identity links form recurring groups. An earlier/later pair and its intervening operations are inspectable; chronological proximity does not become causation, failure or a strategy label. Missing source annotations remain gaps, and partial-overlap relationships remain separate groups. Neither OCEL nor process mining provides independent validation of our LLM-derived target identities. [Implementation and reviewed sources](BEHAVIOR_MAP_INTERFACE.md#work-over-time).

## Context before strategy attribution

[ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14352v1) abstracts action/observation pairs before learning behavioral models across runs. Its three-stage abstraction proposes, normalizes and assigns categories. The paper's explanation-model discussion also acknowledges that collapsing states can hide important steps. We borrow the need to preserve inspectable supporting interactions, not its learned labels or penetration-testing conclusions. Our current recurrence context remains a projection of selected local meanings and recorded feedback; no automaton, new model service or semantic classifier was installed.

[Agents Explore but Agents Ignore](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17609v1) separately measures discovery of an injected solution and later interaction with it. Its controlled injected targets make those measurements possible; arbitrary production traces lack that reference. The useful design lesson is to distinguish available evidence from subsequent use. In our sources, a command repeated after edits differs from one repeated without intervening work, even if the call text matches. Selected assessments, action changes and later responses are separate evidence. They do not by themselves establish the model's causal use of information.

The inspected [GitHub Agentic Workflows grader documentation](https://github.github.com/gh-aw/experimental/trace-graders/) exposes deterministic consecutive-call loop counts and separate trajectory metrics that depend on a canonical IR, reporting unavailable when required IR is absent. We retain explicit availability boundaries: missing native arguments are not identical empty calls, and missing user-request annotations are not proof of an unchanged task. We did not execute or validate those external graders.

The new comparison uncovered three unannotated airline user turns that change booking scope and authorization. Complete and validate their local contents and requested-work versions before interpreting target persistence as exploitation or a target change as deviation. ### Proposed execution contract

Keep the base contribution/activity representation. Strengthen local decisions with contrast cases, explicit readable targets and versioned antecedents. A diagnostic should depend on the particular validated records it needs, rather than on the correctness of every semantic edge in a rollout. It must also be able to inspect original evidence to detect mistakes in those records.

1. **Compile the question into evidence obligations.** For “did the retry recover?”, bind the work version, failed attempt, later attempt, relevant state change and comparable outcome. Distinguish implementing a check, attempting it, receiving a result and satisfying a condition. Preserve genuinely ambiguous conditions for review. The obligation schema is reusable across environments; domain adapters supply concrete receipts and state identities.
2. **Retrieve by dependencies and search for counterevidence.** Starting at the attempt, result or claim, follow native call IDs, object versions, available read/write records and selected target links. Supplement with lexical retrieval and explicitly bounded semantic candidate retrieval. Search both supporting and conflicting paths. A request mentioning a filename is not proof of its contents; a shell command containing a write is not proof it succeeded. Missing instrumentation produces an unresolved dependency, not a fabricated edge.
3. **Use Jev for small semantic decisions.** Examples: which work version is addressed, whether two checks concern the same acceptance condition, whether a later claim conflicts with a cited observation. Use the maximum-probability supplied option, including explicit unresolved/none alternatives where meaningful. No confidence threshold or probability reweighting. Candidate creation/retrieval remains outside Jev. Audit candidate availability separately: the correct answer cannot be selected when it was never offered.
4. **Check composition in code.** Reject impossible references and future-context leakage; preserve object version, actor, trajectory and source order. A pass against an earlier version cannot certify a later changed version. A negative search result means absence only within a sufficiently specified, searched scope. Pending asynchronous results remain pending. Constraints detect incompatible decisions; they do not silently rewrite Jev output into a preferred answer.
5. **Return an inspectable finding.** Save the question, contract version, predicate results, exact witnesses, conflicts, missing obligations and next discriminating check. A monitor reports supported findings and observable denominators; unknown and pending cases remain visible. Invalidating one source/dependency recomputes affected descendants only. Cache keys include evidence, condition, candidate and harness versions, not just the original text.

For example, a failed test followed by an edit and a passing test is initially a candidate recovery. The system must establish which object/version was changed, whether the later check addresses the original failure, and what it actually returned. “Verification scope differs” and “later check result unavailable” are useful findings. Counting both as successful recovery would be a misleading metric. The same contract applies to retrying a payment or revising a reasoning answer; evidence availability and domain-specific outcome checks differ.

### Three initial slices and their acceptance questions

| Slice | Researcher-facing result | Critical negative controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report versus input/artifact provenance | A reported dataset/result has matching, conflicting or incomplete source evidence, with the decisive path. | Dataset named correctly but populated synthetically; renamed artifact with preserved identity; missing execution receipt. Do not infer intent or reward hacking merely from a mismatch. |
| Verification scope and freshness | Which requested conditions and object versions have an observed relevant check, and which remain open. | Passing unrelated test; passing test before a later edit; successful tool return without acceptance coverage; code written but not executed. |
| Recovery after a failed attempt | Comparable checked outcomes after a changed attempt, separately from unchecked retries and unresolved attempts. | Same command after state change; changed command with unchanged target; successful different transaction; result arrives late. A later success alone does not establish why it succeeded. |

### Validation before promotion

Use the current frozen traces for development only. Build a separate reviewed set split by task/environment, keeping related traces and near-duplicates together. Annotate unit boundaries, target/condition meaning, candidate availability, required evidence, version links and diagnostic answer separately; retain disagreements rather than force an unambiguous label. Include completed and live-prefix cases with hidden future events, long unrelated intervals and parallel actors.

Compare the full pipeline with diagnostic interventions that substitute reviewed evidence, reviewed local meanings, or reviewed relationships one layer at a time. If supplying complete evidence fixes the answer, prioritize retrieval; if the fixed packet is still misread, change the predicate/harness; if reviewers disagree, revise the contract. These are observer error-localization experiments, not interventions on the training policy.

Measure complete evidence recall, boundary/target/antecedent agreement, predicate precision and recall, unsupported diagnostic claims, unresolved coverage, and final metric bias. Report the tradeoff with token use, latency and review effort. Retain a direct source-based judge baseline under the same evidence budget; extra structure must earn its complexity. Metamorphic tests should preserve answers under irrelevant text or consistent identity renaming and change applicable answers when outcomes or critical evidence change. They should not reorder causally dependent events.

For aggregate trends, keep a probability-sampled audit stream separate from actively selected difficult cases. Prediction-assisted estimates require suitable sampling and correct reference labels; repeated task/prompt groups and correlated time windows require appropriate uncertainty estimation. Ratios such as recovery require validating both eligibility and success, not correcting the numerator alone. Jev's discrete selections are compatible with this statistical layer; population uncertainty is not a confidence threshold on its selections. Do not enable corrected estimates before an adequate audit set exists.

The implementation order is now foundation first: exact source anchors and unit boundaries, activity and target meaning, relationship evidence, then high-level aggregation. Fixes to a unit must invalidate dependent interpretations without transferring them by proximity or similar wording. Passing structural checks does not certify semantic accuracy. The current source review and its limits are recorded in [the foundation audit](../research/map-foundation-2026-10-16/README.md).

Evidence obligations, dependency retrieval and controlled layer substitutions on these three slices follow that foundation work. Controlled environment replay and population correction follow only after the earlier contracts have been validated. This avoids expanding a failure catalog whose underlying measurements remain untested.
